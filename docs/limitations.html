Known limitations

DX-CAD™ builds every solid with OpenCASCADE (OCCT), the geometry kernel underneath it; its release notes are at github.com/Open-Cascade-SAS/OCCT/releases. This page says plainly what DX-CAD cannot do, or does unreliably, why, and what to do instead. It describes the version you have and promises nothing about later ones. At the end, every case of DX-CAD's robustness suite is listed with what it comes to today, regenerated from the suite itself.

In the diagrams, green is what works and red dashed is what is refused or goes wrong.

Fillets that would consume a face

(Picture: Two 100 mm blocks from the front: 40 mm fillets on the top edges leave a flat top, valid; 50 mm fillets meet in the middle and take the whole top face, refused.)

Cause: a fillet as big as half the face it runs along leaves nothing of that face between its blends, and OCCT cannot build the solid that would need. DX-CAD refuses it and names the radius.

Workaround: make the fillet smaller than half the shortest edge it meets, or remove the face first and model the round as a revolve or a sketch arc.

Fillets meeting at a vertex

(Picture: A 10 mm cube from above: 4 mm fillets on every edge leave flat faces, valid; 5 mm fillets make the blends meet in a point, refused.)

Cause: where three or more filleted edges meet at a corner, OCCT has to build a corner patch between the blends. When the blends meet in a point, or nearly so, there is no patch to build, and the fillet fails.

Workaround: give a radius under half the shortest edge that meets the corner, or fillet fewer edges in one call; or chamfer the corner instead.

Variable-radius fillets

(Picture: One edge side on: a fillet of one radius along the whole edge, valid; a fillet growing from 2 mm to 8 mm, not offered.)

Cause: DX-CAD's fillets take one radius per call, from the model language, the window and the agent door alike; a blend whose radius changes along its edge is not offered.

Workaround: split the edge where the radius should change and fillet each part at its own radius, or model the shape as a loft between sketched sections.

Booleans on coincident or tangent faces

(Picture: Two blocks sharing a face: a hairline gap makes the fuse fail, red dashed; after the retry with a tolerance of 0.00001 mm, one clean solid, green.)

Cause: when two parts share a face, or touch along a line or at a point, floating-point noise can leave a hairline gap or overlap that OCCT reads as two solids. DX-CAD retries a failed boolean once with a fuzzy tolerance of 0.00001 mm (BOOLEAN_FUZZY_VALUE), which closes such gaps; when that fails too, or the parts only touch along a line or at a point, the boolean is refused rather than leaving two solids or an invalid one.

Workaround: make the parts overlap by some volume, at least a few hundredths of a millimetre, rather than only touching.

Shelling and offsets on complex faces

(Picture: A plate with a boss, cut through: a 1 mm wall fits inside the boss, valid; a 3 mm wall is wider than half the boss, so the walls cross, refused.)

Cause: hollowing a part offsets every face inwards by the wall's thickness. Where a feature is thinner than two walls, or a face is a free-form surface whose offset folds over itself, the offset faces cross and OCCT cannot close the shell.

Workaround: give a wall thinner than half the thinnest feature, or hollow the plain body first and add the thin features after.

Persistent naming

(Picture: A plate with one hole, then with a second hole added: the kernel's face 7 moves to the new hole, red dashed; DX-CAD's hole 1 stays on the first, green.)

Cause: OCCT numbers a solid's faces and edges afresh each time it is built, so "face 7" can be a different face after an earlier feature changes. This is the persistent naming problem every CAD kernel has.

Workaround: DX-CAD does not keep kernel numbers between runs. A model is a script, so each feature is built from the values in it, not from picked faces; mates and references name faces by what they are and where they lie ("hole 1", ">Z", a face picked by position), found again on every run; and each part keeps a stable id of its own in the file, whatever it is renamed. Name faces in a model by position, not by number.

STEP and IGES files that need healing

(Picture: A face read from a STEP file whose boundary does not close, red dashed, not a valid solid; the same face healed and closed, green.)

Cause: a STEP file from another program can carry faces whose edges do not quite meet, or solids that are not closed. DX-CAD reads STEP as it is and does not heal it, so such a solid is reported as not valid by OCCT's check, and booleans on it may fail. IGES files are not read at all.

Workaround: heal or sew the model in the program that wrote it and export it again as closed solids, in STEP (AP214 or AP242).

No simultaneous assembly solver

(Picture: Three parts: B placed against A by mate 1, then C against B by mate 2, green; a mate from C back to A closes a loop, red dashed, refused.)

Cause: DX-CAD resolves an assembly's mates one at a time, in the order the model writes them, each placing its part against parts already placed. A mate that would need several parts moved at once, such as one closing a loop, or one that disagrees with the mates before it, is refused rather than solved.

Workaround: write the mates in the order the parts are placed, each part mated to parts before it, and change the order by editing the script.

Large assemblies

(Picture: An assembly of a few dozen parts, quick, green; thousands of parts, each its own shape in the view, slow, red dashed.)

Cause: every part is its own full shape in the view and in the file, with no lightweight stand-ins, so an assembly of thousands of parts is slow to open, to turn and to save.

Workaround: keep sub-assemblies in files of their own and bring them in with use(), working on one at a time.

Pictures without a window on macOS

(Picture: A part's picture drawn with no window open: drawn on Windows and on Linux with a display, green; on macOS the request is refused, red dashed.)

Cause: a picture drawn with no window open (dxcad render, the agent's render_view, the gallery, and the preview a saved part carries for Explorer) needs a hidden native window for OCCT to draw into. On macOS that is a Cocoa window, made on the application's main thread, which DX-CAD does not make without its own window. Such a request is refused there, saying so, and a part saved on macOS has no preview picture.

Workaround: open the part in DX-CAD's window to see it, or draw its picture on Windows or on Linux. With the window open, the Assistant's render_view and the window link's draw from the window's own view on every platform (item 771), so the Assistant looks at the model on a Mac as it does elsewhere; only a picture asked for with no window open is refused there.

The robustness suite

The hard cases DX-CAD is tested against each time, each run as a model.py: booleans of coplanar and tangent faces, blends bigger than their edges allow, shells against what they meet, overlapping patterns, sheet bends of radius 0, tiny text and mates that cannot be met. Each ends in a valid solid or is refused with its reason and what would work instead, never a crash. This list is rewritten by python -m dxcad.docsgen limitations, and the test suite fails when it and the cases disagree.

Run on OCCT 7.9.3, each case a model.py as dxcad run runs one.
